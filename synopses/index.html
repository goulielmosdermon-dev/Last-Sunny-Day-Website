LAST SUNNY DAY

The sun rises over the Aegean Sea. On the deck of a ferry, Oliver wakes up from a dream about a bullet going through a chicken’s head. Next to him is Giorgio, his brother in everything but blood. Two Sicilian hitmen, dressed as pallbearers in cheap black suits, sunglasses on, heading to a Greek island neither has ever seen.

By 7am they’re in a cheap motel. Oliver paces the room, rehearsing how to tell Giorgio he wants out. He can’t get past the first sentence. Giorgio comes back, and they get ready.

The job is easy. A traitor hiding on a rotting boat. They find him, shoot him and haul him to a funeral home, where Frank, the man who raised them into killers and a mafia boss running an underground organisation of funeral homes, is waiting. While they chop up the body, Frank sends them on one more last-minute errand. Pick up a dead dancer named Anna from a strip club run by a coke-addicted guy who performs as Elvis.

So the boys visit the Club, pick up the woman, put her in the coffin, and drive hat they don’t know is that, hiding in a closet, her 15-year-old daughter, Lydia, watches them take her mother away.

The midday sun is brutal, and Giorgio wants a swim, but Oliver insists the job is finished first. In these kinds of arguments, Giorgio’s persistent personality always wins, and so it does. Oliver stops the car; they strip down to their underwear and walk down to the beach.

As they wrestle in the waves, we drift back to Sicily to meet their past, how they became men at the local whorehouse, and how they were turned into killers by Frank.

Back in the present, after the swim, they walk on the scorching sand only to realise the hearse is gone from their horizon.

What started as an easy mission has turned into a lost car with a dead body inside, their clothes and weapons included. Now semi-naked and stranded, they have to act fast and unnoticed.

They hitch a ride on the back of a farmer’s truck, sitting between cages of chickens. Oliver locks eyes with one of them, which brings back an mory. Then, passing by a gas station in the middle of nowhere, they spot the hearse parked with no one inside.

Before they can get close, Lydia steps out, holding their spare gun, and opens fire. A bullet grazes Oliver’s forehead; she gets in the car and drives away.

At this point, we cut to black and drift back once more, this time into Lydia’s past. Born in that club to a mother who was barely a child herself. Raised between the stage, the drugs, and the red rooms. Watching her mother slowly disappear into heroin while Elvis, the man who took them in, prepared Lydia to take her place.

We come back to the present, back at the gas station, while Oliver walks out of the bathroom having just patched up his wound; we see the attendant dead behind the counter and Giorgio waiting outside with a smile on his face.

Back on the road, the plan now is simple: steal a ride and find the girl and the car. Oliver is the bait while Giorgio is hidden behind some trees, waiting for the right time to attack the drivehe plan goes south when a young cop stops Oliver and starts asking too many questions, eventually talking him into getting in the car for questioning at the station.

As Oliver sits patiently in the police car, he and the cop notice a body on the street. It’s Giorgio pretending to be dead. As the cop walks closer and kneels over, Oliver shoots him in the head with the cop’s pistol.

The day is getting worse by the hour.

Burnt by the sun and bleeding from their feet, the boys walk through fields and ancient ruins until they reach a small church. Oliver goes inside alone and makes a promise to God. He will never take another life. If he breaks it, God can send him straight to hell.

They break into an empty villa to find clothes, and there Oliver finally says what he’s been holding in all day. He’s done.
Giorgio can’t accept it. He storms out and goes back to Frank, who gives him one order. Take Oliver out.

Meanwhile, Lydia, lost with a coffin she can’t even lift out of the car to bury her mothest Oliver sitting alone on the side of the road.

They barely share a language, but they understand each other. Together they push her mother’s coffin off a cliff and into the sea. On the sand, Lydia teaches him an old Greek poem her mother used to read her at night. Sweet is life, and death is darkness.

As the sun goes down, Giorgio walks into the motel room with his gun drawn. He points it at his sleeping brother, then lowers it. Frank won’t be a problem anymore. Giorgio chose Oliver.

That night, the boys sit in the club and watch Greek Elvis give the show of his life. Backstage, Oliver breaks his promise to God and kills him, setting Lydia free from the man who destroyed her mother. In the hallway, Elvis’s bodyguard is waiting with a shotgun. He shoots Oliver. Giorgio shoots the bodyguard, but it’s too late.

At dawn, Giorgio carries him to the edge of a cliff to watch one last sunrise.

I’ll see you in hell, brother. Oliver dies.
