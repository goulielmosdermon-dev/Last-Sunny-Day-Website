Francesco Pistone Attached

Late 20s. Italian. A quiet storm behind dark sunglasses. Oliver moves like someone carrying a ghost—measured, watchful, haunted by stillness. His illness has dulled the sharpness of his hitman edge, replacing it with melancholy introspection. Beneath the black suit and cigarette smoke is a boy who once loved gently, now trapped between duty and death. Thoughtful and empathetic, but capable of lethal precision, Oliver is a man trying to make peace with the past before it buries him.

Giacomo Giorgio Attached

Male, late 20s, Italian. Giorgio is volatile, expressive, and emotionally raw. He covers his wounds with swagger, jokes, and sudden violence. Beneath his bravado is a desperate need for connection.
More impulsive than Oliver and more broken than he admits, Giorgio is loyal to a fault. He’s like a stray dog who found a pack. Torn between tenderness and brutality, Giorgio carries his trauma with the charm of a boy pretending not to care.

Once trafficked, now escaped, she moves like a ghost reclaiming her own story. Her presence disrupts everything. She is the moral center of the film—a mirror to the men’s brutality, and a force with more to lose, and more courage to act.

Giannis Klinis Attached

Male, 40s–50s, Greek. A grotesque relic of showbiz and sleaze. Greek Elvis is trapped in a fantasy version of himself, a tragic parody of faded fame. Rhinestones, sweat, and menace define his surface. Beneath it, he is both predator and coward—hiding cruelty behind flamboyant theatrics.
To outsiders, he is a joke. To those under his control, he is a nightmare. His glittering stage persona masks a violent core. He is entertainment turned rotten, a false smile painted on an abuser still desperate for applause.
